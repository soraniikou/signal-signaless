宮沢賢治の童話『シグナルとシグナレス』は、鉄道本線の新型信号機「シグナル」と軽便鉄道の旧型信号機「シグナレス」の恋物語です。　

金属製で電気式の立派な信号機であるシグナルは、少し離れたところにある木製でランプ式の信号機シグナレスに恋をし、熱烈な愛の言葉を贈ります。シグナレスもまたシグナルに惹かれており、二人はお互いを深く想い合うようになります。ところが、この結婚に本線側の電信柱などが大反対します。身分違いの恋であることや、シグナレスの微笑みがどんな意味だったかを電信柱が東京を経由して本線に告げ口したことなどがきっかけで、二人は引き裂かれそうになります。一歩も動けない信号機である二人は駆け落ちすることもできず、結婚を阻まれて苦悩します。
光での融合: 最後に二人の放つシグナル（青い光と緑の光）が夜空のなかで美しく交わり、精神的・宇宙的な次元で一つに結ばれました。

この物語の最後に僕たちは星になっていつまでも青い光の中で更新するという魂の約束が交わされます。物理的には交わらない切なさを感じます。

Kenji Miyazawa’s fairy tale "Signal and Signalles" is a love story between "Signal," a new traffic signal on the main railway line, and "Signalles," an older signal on a light railway.

Signal, a grand electric signal made of metal, falls in love with Signalles, a wooden, lamp-lit signal standing a short distance away, and expresses his passionate love for her. Signalles is equally drawn to Signal, and the two develop a deep, mutual affection.

However, their union is met with fierce opposition, particularly from the telegraph poles on the main line. Deeming it a love between different social classes, the telegraph poles report Signalles’s gentle smile back to the main line via Tokyo, threatening to tear the two apart. Bound to their spots and unable to move, the two signals cannot run away together, left only to suffer as their marriage is forbidden.

Yet, in the end, the signals they send out—a blue light and a green light—blend beautifully in the night sky, uniting them on a spiritual and cosmic level.

At the very end of this story, they make a soulful promise: to become stars and continue to renew themselves within the blue light forever. It evokes a poignant sadness, knowing they can never physically touch.

While one might view this as a painful, negative rift, I like to believe that how we imagine their future is left freely to each of our hearts.

I once learned from a Japanese artist that light shines through from the deep end of a painful rift.🤟 How we choose to perceive even a rift, and how we imagine what lies beyond it, is entirely up to our own hearts.
